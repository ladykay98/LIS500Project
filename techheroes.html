Our Tech Heroes

Kamaria's Tech Hero

In today’s technological age, we often take the word "computer" for granted. We see them simply as metal boxes full of circuits and algorithms that help us write papers, surf the web, and design presentations. But as our technology shifts from static hand calculations to automated artificial intelligence, the definition of what powers these systems—and who oversees them—is changing rapidly. One of my modern tech heroes is Dr. Timnit Gebru, a computer scientist and pioneer in AI ethics whose work focuses on holding tech giants accountable for the automated tools they deploy. After earning her Ph.D. at Stanford and working as a senior researcher, she became a prominent co-lead at Google's Ethical AI team. There, she co-authored groundbreaking research exposing how commercial facial recognition systems had alarming error rates for women of color and how massive AI models carry hidden societal and environmental costs.
Dr. Gebru was dubbed a "Google whistleblower" after she was ousted from the company for refusing to retract her research on the dangers of large language models (LLMs). But rather than being silenced, she doubled down on her mission. She actively challenges the popular corporate narrative that LLMs represent some inevitable "super-intelligence." Instead, she points out that framing AI as an all-powerful, sentient force is largely a marketing ploy—a clever distraction designed to help tech companies bypass real government regulation. She argues that by convincing the public to focus on abstract "doomsday" scenarios, these corporations attempt to set their own self-serving guardrails while avoiding immediate accountability for the real-world harms their software unleashes right now. Navigating corporate pressure while standing up to some of the most powerful tech monopolies in history requires immense courage and conviction.
Today, Dr. Gebru continues this mission through the Distributed AI Research Institute (DAIR), an independent organization she founded to conduct AI research free from big-tech influence. She is a true pioneer for ethical engineering in the modern digital age.

Some of her most notable accomplishments include:

  • Co-Founding Black in AI: Created a global community to increase representation, mentorship, and research opportunities for Black researchers in artificial intelligence.
  • Pioneering "Datasheets for Datasets": Developed a standardized documentation framework (inspired by electronics engineering) to force transparency around how AI training data is collected and audited.
  • Exposing Algorithmic Bias: Published the landmark Gender Shades study with Dr. Joy Buolamwini, proving severe demographic disparities in commercial computer vision tools.
  • Founding DAIR: Established an independent, public-interest research institute designed to build transparent AI models rooted in community needs rather than corporate profit.

    As I look toward a future career in public service and technology policy, Dr. Gebru’s work serves as a powerful model. She proves that true innovation isn't just about building faster algorithms—it’s about having the integrity to ensure those systems serve humanity safely, equitably, and transparently.

Mike's Tech Hero: Maria Ressa

As a former journalist and someone who’s interested in disinformation, when I reflect on the concept of a tech hero, I don’t necessarily think of s omeone who knows how to code let alone create a product. I think about those who hold Big Tech accountable and making sure that modern technology is safe for everyone to use.

Through that lens, one of my tech heroes is Maria Ressa, a Filipina journalist and Nobel Peace Prize laureate. She is the CEO of Rappler, a Filipino digital news outlet. In 2016, she investigated how then Philippine President Rodrigo Duterte used social media to spread disinformation by using paid followers and dummy accounts. Ressa ultimately became a target for the Duterte administration and looked for ways to punish her. She was eventually arrested for “cyber libel.” Her arrest was criticized by various human rights organizations.

I’ve seen interviews with Maria Ressa on various shows, including The Daily Show with Jon Stewart. But I was impacted by her book, How to Stand Up to a Dictator, which talks about her experience battling the Duterte administration and saw parallels on what is going on in America with the Trump administrations.

In her book, she tried to hold Mark Zuckerberg accountable for Facebook as a tool for spreading disinformation, which led to the election of Duterte. Those disinformation campaigns in the Philippines were replicated on a grander scale in the UK with Brexit and the first election of Donald Trump.
